## Findings

1. **MISMATCH: Q readback is misstated.** `language/pasm2/concepts/special_registers.yaml:44` says *"access: Via GETQX/GETQY after CORDIC, SETQ/SETQ2 for block ops"* and :46 *"uses: CORDIC result retrieval"*. SD:1181 says *"The SETQ and SETQ2 instructions write to the Q register"*, and SD:1191-1194 says *"It is possible to retrieve the current Q value by the following sequence: MOV qval,#0 / MUXQ qval,##$FFFFFFFF"*. GETQX/GETQY return CORDIC X/Y, not Q. An agent would write `GETQX` to read Q. §4 extends this page but does not name this line.
2. **NOT-CARRIED: MUXQ Q-readback idiom** (SD:1191-1194). Searches: `MUXQ.*FFFFFFFF`, `qval`, `read Q`, `current Q`, `retrieve.*Q`, `readback`. No hit. Homes: `special_registers.yaml` q_register, `muxq.yaml`.
3. **MISMATCH: EXECF order reversed.** `language/pasm2/concepts/branch_addressing.yaml:84` says *"Execute fast - skip instructions then branch"* and :86 says *"Load skip pattern, execute, then branch"*. SD:786 says *"jump to D[9:0] in cog/LUT and initiate SKIPF using D[31:10]"*, and SD:864 says *"performs a JMP and a SKIPF at the same time"*. With the YAML's order, the pattern would apply to the instructions after EXECF rather than at the target. `execf.yaml`, `instruction_skipping.yaml:54` and `skipf_branching.yaml:25` are right.
4. **MISMATCH: REP "zero overhead" stated unscoped.** `language/pasm2/concepts/rep_instruction.yaml:83-84` says *"Subsequent iterations: Zero overhead / Total cycles: (block_cycles × count) + 2"*. SD:773 says *"REP works in hub memory, as well, but executes a hidden jump to get back to the top"*. `cog_hub_execution.yaml:32-34` says it costs 13+ clocks per iteration. §3 adds only a link, which leaves the contradicting timing line in place.
5. **NOT-CARRIED: how to single-step XBYTE** (SD:1137-1177): the software emulation with the stack stuffed with `byteloop`, a 21-NOP landing strip for trailing skip patterns, and RFBYTE PA / GETPTR PB / RDLUT / EXECF. `architecture/xbyte_engine.yaml:315` says only *"trace_mode: Can single-step bytecodes"*, which implies native support. Searches: `landing strip`, `21-NOP`, `single-step.*XBYTE`, `XBYTE.*single`.
6. **MISMATCH (minor): XBYTE `%x` legend.** `architecture/xbyte_engine.yaml:117-119` says *"%x = mode-operand bit 1 ... in the smaller tables bit 1 is the index-form selector"*. In SD Table 9 (SD:1004-1042), `%AAxx0010F`, `%AAAx1010F` and `%AAAAx100F` place the x bits at bits 6:5, 5 and 4 (don't-care base bits), and bit 1 is a fixed 0/1 selector. Only `%A000000xF` (SD:988) has x at bit 1. The index_form_bit conclusion is right; the legend's definition of `%x` is wrong.
7. **MISMATCH (minor): XBYTE routines "in LUT".** `architecture/xbyte_engine.yaml:8` says *"Executes bytecode routines from LUT"* and :14 *"routine_storage: LUT RAM (512 longs)"*. SD:963 says the LUT holds the translation table, *"where the 10 LSBs are an address to jump to in cog/LUT RAM"*. The page's own :228 says *"Must be in COG or LUT RAM"*.
8. **PARTIAL contract rows worth carrying:** CRCNIB protection by STALLI/ALLOWI or REP, and why (SD:1189; `crcnib.yaml:12`). The cog/hub `#@` worked table (SD:728-751), in particular that hub-code `JMP #@cog` is hub-to-hub relative. The 13-clock EXECF loop figure (SD:874). The relative-branch bytecode pattern in the XBYTE demo (SD:1097-1099, :1109).

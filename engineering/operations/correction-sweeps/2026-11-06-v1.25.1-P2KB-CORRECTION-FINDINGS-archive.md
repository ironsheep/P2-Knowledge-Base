## Found by the RFVAR/RFVARS change «#440» (2026-11-05) — F-632, F-633

### F-632 — the REP pages do not carry the Silicon Doc's account of REP and interrupts — `RESOLVED` (found 2026-11-05, «#440», live validation of v1.25.0; released KB v1.25.1, verified live 2026-11-06)
Propeller 2 Documentation v35 states it in three places: INSTRUCTION REPEATING ("Interrupts will be ignored during REP looping"), SETQ CONSIDERATIONS (a REP block "is automatically shielded from interrupts, including non-stallable debug interrupts") and INTERRUPTS ("REP must not be executing or active" before the branch can happen; "Once these conditions are all met, any pending interrupt is allowed to branch"). The three CORDIC pages said REP stalls interrupts, and the REP pages carried only the first statement. Spin2 v55 and v51 do say "use REP to stall all interrupts (including debug)", so "stall" was not unsourced. **Stephen, 2026-11-06: "write the YAML changes as close as possible to what the Silicon Doc says. We trust the Silicon Doc."**
> **Applied 2026-11-05, reworded 2026-11-06:** `rep.yaml` and `rep_instruction.yaml` carry all three statements in the Silicon Doc's words; `qmul.yaml` and `cordic_solver.yaml` use "shielded" and the pending branch; `rep_instruction.yaml`'s `.stall` label and `extended_interrupt_stall` key become `.protect` and `extended_interrupt_protection`, and "Interrupt-free zone" and "Cannot be interrupted mid-block" go (`_ret_` is a branch, INSTRUCTION SKIPPING, so the REP-ends-at-`_ret_` example stands); `cordic_solver.yaml`'s "Execute block atomically" comment and `event_interrupt_config.yaml`'s "REP blocks delay interrupts" follow the source. The SETQ twin: `event_interrupt_config.yaml` "SETQ blocks are atomic" and `setq_block_ops.yaml` "Block transfers are atomic" now say what SETQ CONSIDERATIONS says. `setq_block_ops.yaml`'s `debug_impact`, `consideration` ("Keep transfers small") and "Interrupts delayed during transfer" lines go because the Silicon Doc does not state them. "Held off" stays in the `deferral_conditions` lists (`interrupts.yaml`, `cog.yaml`, `event_system.yaml`, `event_interrupt_config.yaml`): those lists restate the INTERRUPTS conditions and agree with it. Class searched: `grep -rnEi 'stall(s|ing)? (all )?interrupts?|interrupt[a-z_ ]*stall'` and `\bREP\b` with interrupt, stall or shield, over `deliverables/ai/P2`. Left: `event_interrupt_config.yaml:64` (it is STALLI itself), `p2an002` ("let an interrupt stall a pipelined sequence", a different meaning), `cog_hub_execution.yaml` and `spin2-pasm2-integration.yaml` (other subjects). Checked, not a defect: `locktry.yaml` and `lockrel.yaml` lock 15 examples quote the debugger's own code with their citation and do not tell a reader to take the lock.
> **Closed live, 2026-11-06:** `p2kb_get` after a flush serves the new wording on all six pages. No bench test of an interrupt during REP exists; the wording rests on the document, by the ruling above. Two leftovers seen in the live read are F-636.

### F-633 — `fvar.yaml` does not say an `FVAR`/`FVARS` value cannot use a label defined later in the file — `RESOLVED` (found 2026-11-05, «#440», compiling the XBYTE §15.4 fix; released KB v1.25.1, verified live 2026-11-06)
CP-027: a label on the next line stops the build with `Undefined symbol`; a CON constant, including one defined after the DAT, and earlier labels are sized normally. The PNut-TS reference does not state it.
> **Applied 2026-11-05, narrowed 2026-11-06:** `rules` carries the limit, and Stephen kept it (2026-11-06). It first said "a symbol defined later"; he questioned the sentence, and compiling a later CON constant showed it compiles, so the limit is on labels. `directives.md` had the same wording and is fixed for Assembly v3.1.12; XBYTE §15.4 already said "label".
> **Closed live, 2026-11-06:** `p2kb_get` after a flush serves "A label defined later in the file stops the build". An auditor's further compiles of about 30 cases found no exception; inherited `obj.CONST` is untested.
